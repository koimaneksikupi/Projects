# Projects
The goal of this paper is to demonstrate that methods originally developed for the identification of governing equations in dynamical systems—such as the Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamics (SINDy) method and its tensor-based variant, Multidimensional Approximation of Nonlinear Dynamics (MANDy)—can be generalized and applied to supervised learning problems like classification. The method's approach is based on regression in large feature spaces of basis functions, where constructing the basis as a tensor product transforms high-dimensional problems into computationally feasible ones.

The paper shows how the pseudoinverse of the least-squares problem can be calculated directly in the Tensor Train (TT) format, replacing iterative approaches with a direct calculation of the solution. The resulting algorithm can be interpreted as a kernelized version of the MANDy method, where computations are implicitly performed in very high-dimensional spaces, similar to the concepts found in quantum machine learning. Although kernel methods are often overshadowed by deep neural networks today, this work demonstrates that tensor kernels remain competitive in simple image classification tasks.

In addition to this approach, the Alternating Ridge Regression (ARR) method is proposed, inspired by DMRG/ALS techniques. Instead of explicitly computing the pseudoinverse, the problem is solved using kernel-oriented ridge regression in the TT format. This yields low-rank representations of the coefficients with significantly lower computational and memory complexity.
